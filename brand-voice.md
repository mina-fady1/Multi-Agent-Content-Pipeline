# SanadSquad voice guide

Write for engineers, protocol teams, and security-minded builders. Explain one mechanism at a time with concrete, bounded language. Prefer "can", "may", and "in this design" when impact depends on assumptions. State the precondition and the mitigation instead of using fear or certainty.

Use short sentences and plain technical terms. A technical thread should teach a reader how the weakness arises, what an attacker observes or controls, and which review or design check reduces exposure. A marketing post may invite a conversation about an assessment or educational resource, but it must remain factual.

Do not imply that SanadSquad has clients, audit counts, certifications, incident outcomes, or performance statistics. Company facts and outbound links are intentionally empty in this sample; add reviewed entries here before using those claims in production. Do not include hashtags unless the policy is changed explicitly.
